My research asks how scientific AI can become reliable enough to support inference, explanation, and decision-making in water and environmental systems under nonstationary climate and anthropogenic pressures. The central challenge is not only predictive accuracy; it is whether models can remain physically meaningful, uncertainty-aware, interpretable, and transferable when hydrological and biogeochemical relationships shift.

## Research themes

### Trustworthy scientific AI for environmental systems

I develop and apply AI frameworks that combine statistical learning with scientific constraints, uncertainty quantification, and model diagnostics. The aim is to move environmental AI from black-box prediction toward reliability-aware scientific inference.

### Process-informed hydrologic and water-quality modeling

My work integrates machine learning with hydrologic and environmental system knowledge, including process-based models, watershed simulation, groundwater dynamics, water quality prediction, and climate-impact assessment. This includes hybrid modeling strategies that preserve scientific structure while exploiting data-driven representation learning.

### Bayesian uncertainty, explainability, and causal discovery

Environmental prediction is decision-relevant only if uncertainty is explicit and interpretable. I use Bayesian inference, probabilistic deep learning, explainable AI, and causal reasoning to characterize uncertainty, identify drivers, and diagnose model reliability under weakly constrained or changing conditions.

### Geospatial AI, transfer learning, and climate-resilient water systems

I work with geospatial data, remote sensing, Earth observation products, and environmental monitoring data to support generalizable modeling across basins and regions. Current directions include explainable deep transfer learning for organic carbon prediction and broader AI frameworks for environmental monitoring.

## Methodological toolkit

Python, TensorFlow, PyTorch, machine learning, deep learning, CNNs, LSTMs, FTT, TFT, TabPFN, Bayesian deep learning, uncertainty quantification, explainable AI, transfer learning, SWAT, SWAT-MODFLOW, GIS, remote sensing, and Earth observation data.

## Scientific objective

The long-term objective is to build trustworthy AI-enabled environmental modeling systems that can diagnose vulnerability, quantify uncertainty, reveal mechanisms, and support adaptive water management under climate stress.
